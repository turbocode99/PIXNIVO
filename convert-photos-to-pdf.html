How to Convert Photos to PDF on Your Phone

2 August 2026•Updated 23 October 2026•5 min read

Someone asks you to "send it as a PDF", and what you have is four photos of a document on your phone. It happens constantly — bank forms, rental agreements, school records, insurance claims, receipts for expenses — and the usual reaction is to install an app full of adverts that wants an account before it will save anything.

You don't need an app. This guide shows how to take photos that make a clean, readable PDF and how to turn them into one file in your phone's browser, without uploading them anywhere.

Why people ask for a PDF instead of photos

  • One file instead of many. A six-page contract sent as six images easily arrives out of order, or with one missing.
  • A fixed page order. A PDF keeps pages exactly in the sequence you set.
  • Predictable printing. PDF pages have real dimensions such as A4 or US Letter, so they print at the right size.
  • Portal rules. Many upload forms only accept PDF for official documents.

Step 1: Take good photos of the paperwork

A PDF made from poor photos is still a poor document. A minute of care here saves a rejected application later.

  • Use daylight from the side. Stand so neither you nor your phone casts a shadow across the page. A window to one side is ideal.
  • Shoot straight down. Hold the phone parallel to the page. Shooting at an angle turns rectangles into trapezoids and makes the far edge blurry.
  • Put the page on a dark surface. White paper on a dark table gives clear edges, which makes cropping easy.
  • Fill the frame. Leave a small margin around the page, but no desk, fingers or furniture.
  • Switch the flash off. Flash creates a bright hotspot, especially on glossy paper or laminated cards.
  • Flatten the page. Folds and curled edges throw parts of the text out of focus. Press the page flat or weigh down the corners.
  • One page per photo. Two pages in one photo means each page gets half the detail.

Check before you leave the table: zoom into each photo on your phone and read the smallest print — dates, reference numbers, footnotes. If you can't read it, neither can the recipient.

If a photo came out sideways or needs trimming, fix it in the Photo Editor first: use the rotate buttons, and crop away the table around the page.

Step 2: Create the PDF

  1. Open Images to PDF in your phone's browser and add your photos. JPG, PNG, WebP and iPhone HEIC are all accepted, and you can add as many as you like — use Add more if you forgot one.
  2. Put the pages in order by dragging them or using the arrows.
  3. Choose a Page size:
    • Fit to image — each page is exactly the size of its photo, with no white borders. Good for photos and mixed shapes.
    • A4 or US Letter — standard paper sizes, best for forms and anything that will be printed.
  4. Leave Orientation on Automatic so each page matches its photo, or force portrait or landscape.
  5. Add Margins (0–30 mm) if the document will be printed and you don't want text right at the edge.
  6. Set Image quality — 85% by default — and tap Create PDF.

A single photo becomes a PDF with the same name; several become one file named after the page count. The tool shows the final size so you know immediately whether it fits an email or portal limit. Photos are processed in your browser and never uploaded.

To keep files manageable, each photo is scaled so its longest side is at most 2,400 pixels — about 200 DPI on an A4 page, which is sharp for both reading and printing.

If the PDF is still too large, lower the quality in steps of about 10% and check the smallest text each time. For a file that's already been created, Compress PDF can shrink it further.

Your phone's built-in scanner is an alternative

Both major phone platforms include a document scanner that straightens pages automatically: on iPhone it's in the Notes and Files apps, and on Android it's in the Google Drive app. They're excellent for paper documents, especially when you can't shoot perfectly straight.

Images to PDF is handier when you already have the photos — including screenshots, HEIC photos and images sent to you by others — when you want control over page size, margins and file size, or when you'd rather not save personal documents to a cloud account.

Common problems

  • Pages are in the wrong order: reorder the list before creating the PDF. The list order is the page order.
  • A page is sideways: rotate the photo in the Photo Editor, then add it again.
  • The text is hard to read: the original photo was too dark, too angled or out of focus. Retake it; no setting can add detail that isn't there.
  • The file is too big: lower Image quality, or check for a photo that's much larger than the others.
  • The recipient wants separate files: create one PDF per document instead of one for everything.

Frequently asked questions

Does this work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. It runs in Safari, Chrome and other modern mobile browsers. Pick photos straight from your gallery; nothing needs installing.

Can I combine the new PDF with existing PDFs?

Yes. Create the PDF from your photos, then combine it with other files using Merge PDF. How to merge PDFs without uploading them explains ordering and troubleshooting.

Will the text in the PDF be searchable?

No — pages made from photos are images, so the text can't be selected. If you need editable or searchable text, run the PDF or the photos through AI OCR.

Is it safe for ID documents?

The conversion happens on your phone, so the photos aren't sent to PIXNIVO or anyone else. As with any sensitive document, share the finished PDF only with the person or organisation that needs it.
