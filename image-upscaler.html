How it works

Honest enlargement, without the blocky edges

Images are enlarged in several small high-quality steps instead of one harsh jump, then an unsharp mask restores edge crispness. It can't invent detail that was never captured, but it makes the detail you have look its best.

Best results

Sharp photos, logos, flat graphics and screenshots of text at 2× — and often at 4×.

Limited results

Tiny thumbnails, motion blur and heavily compressed JPGs — artifacts get enlarged too.

How to upscale an image

Enlarge a photo, logo or screenshot in six steps, then check the result with the before-and-after slider.

  1. Add the best copy you have. Drop in or paste the original file. A copy that has been through a messaging app or a screenshot carries compression artifacts, and enlarging makes them bigger.
  2. Choose a scale. 2× works well for most images; 3× and 4× suit sharp originals. 1× keeps the size and applies only sharpening and tone — useful for a slightly soft photo that's already big enough.
  3. Set the sharpening. The default strength of 55 suits most photos. Lower it if you see bright halos along edges or grainy skin.
  4. Adjust tone if needed. A small contrast boost to 105–115% often adds more apparent crispness than extra sharpening.
  5. Compare. Drag the slider across the image. If you can't see an improvement, reduce the settings rather than pushing harder.
  6. Export. Choose PNG for logos, graphics and screenshots, or JPG or WebP for photos to keep the file smaller.

How large should the result be?

For a sharp print, you need about 300 pixels per inch: a 6 × 4 inch print needs 1,800 × 1,200 pixels, and A4 needs about 2,480 × 3,508. For screens, 1,920 pixels wide fills a Full HD display. Enlarge only as far as you need; output is capped at 40 megapixels to keep your browser responsive.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an AI upscaler?

No. It uses careful multi-step resampling and an unsharp mask instead of a generative AI model. That means it never invents details such as text, faces or textures that weren't in your image — the result stays faithful to the original. The trade-off is that it can't recreate detail that was never captured.

Are my images uploaded?

No. Upscaling runs entirely in your browser, so your images stay on your device.

Can I upscale a small logo for printing?

For simple logos with flat colours, 2× to 4× often works well. For the best print quality, ask for the original vector file (SVG, EPS or PDF), or design a new logo in the Logo Generator and export it at 2,048 px from the start.

Why does my upscaled photo look over-processed?

The sharpening is probably too strong for that image. Lower Sharpen until halos disappear, and add a little contrast instead.
