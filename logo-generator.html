Guide

Tips for a logo that works everywhere

A good logo is simple, readable at small sizes and consistent wherever it appears. Use these guidelines while you design.

Designing in the studio

  1. Describe one clear idea. A single subject — "mountain peak", "coffee cup", "leaf" — makes a stronger symbol than a combination of several. Pick the visual style that suits your brand.
  2. Set your brand name and an optional tagline. Keep the tagline short; it becomes unreadable at small sizes.
  3. Choose typography. Pick a font, then adjust the name size and letter spacing. Wider spacing suits short names in capitals; long names usually need less.
  4. Pick a layout and frame. Stacked places the name under the symbol, Horizontal puts them side by side for website headers, and Arc curves the text. Frames — circle, squircle, shield or hexagon — turn the logo into a badge.
  5. Set colours and the artboard. Preview the logo on dark, light and gradient backgrounds; choose Transparent before downloading if you need no background.
  6. Export. Download a 2,048 px PNG for print and social media, an SVG with the name and frame as vector shapes, or a 512 px app icon.

Rules of thumb from professional designers

  • Test it small. Shrink the preview to the size of a browser tab icon. If the symbol turns into a blob or the name is unreadable, simplify.
  • Limit the palette. One or two colours plus a neutral are easier to reproduce on signs, print and embroidery.
  • Check it in one colour. A logo that still works in plain black or white will survive faxes, stamps and engraving.
  • Export large and keep the originals. Save the 2,048 px PNG and the SVG as your master files, and make smaller copies from them rather than enlarging a small image later.

Frequently asked questions

Which file format should I use?

Use the 2,048 px PNG for social media profiles, documents, print and anything that doesn't accept SVG; the 512 px app icon for app stores and home-screen icons; and the SVG for websites and design tools. In the SVG, the brand name, tagline and frame are vector shapes that stay sharp at any size, while the symbol is embedded as a high-resolution image.

Can I use the logo for my business?

PIXNIVO's tools are free for personal and commercial use. Before adopting a logo as your brand, search your country's trademark database to make sure nothing similar is already registered in your industry — simple symbols can resemble existing marks.

How do I get a logo with no background?

Select Transparent in the artboard options before downloading. Both the PNG and the SVG then have no background.

Can I edit the logo later?

Yes. The SVG opens in vector editors such as Inkscape, Figma or Illustrator, where you can change the text, fonts, colours and frame. The symbol is an embedded image, so to change it, generate a new one in the studio and export again.
